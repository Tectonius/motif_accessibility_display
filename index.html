About these figures

Aggregate profile (top)

Generated with deepTools computeMatrix reference-point + plotProfile. The x-axis is distance in bp from the reference point (0 = the reference point itself — see below for what that point is in each analysis). The y-axis is the mean signal value, averaged across every region in the set, at that position. It's a single aggregate line — it doesn't show how much individual regions vary from each other or from one another; that's what the heatmap is for.

Heatmap (bottom)

Same underlying matrix as the profile plot, rendered with plotHeatmap instead of averaged down to one line. Same x-axis (distance from the reference point). Each row is one individual region — one ChIP-seq peak, or one motif instance — not an aggregate of anything. Color encodes that specific region's signal intensity at that bin. Rows are sorted top-to-bottom by descending mean signal (deepTools' default --sortRegions descend --sortUsing mean), so the top rows are the individual sites with the strongest, most consistent signal, fading to the weakest/noisiest sites at the bottom. That ordering is a sort, not a biological gradient — there's no reason to expect anything continuous moving down the axis, since each row is an unrelated site.

Motif accessibility vs. Nucleosome detection

Motif accessibility: regions are JASPAR motif instances for the selected TF, filtered to only the instances that overlap a real ChIP-seq peak for that TF ("motif-verified ChIP-seq peaks") — centered on the motif's own predicted location, ±3000bp flank, 25bp bins. Nucleosome detection: regions are the TF's raw ChIP-seq peaks with no motif filtering ("ChIP-seq peaks only"), centered on the peak's own summit — used to look for the periodic nucleosome-phasing ripples that flank a strongly bound site, ±2000bp flank, 10bp bins. Because the reference point, flank, and bin size all differ between the two, they aren't directly comparable position-for-position, and (see the sort tooltip above) their oscillation scores are computed independently rather than shared.

Full vs. DNase-I only track

Both tracks are the same kind of measurement — a base-pair-resolution pileup of enzymatic cut/insertion events, additively pooled across GM12878 Hi-C replicates. Full is all 237 pooled replicates, which ENCODE metadata shows span at least six different fragmentation chemistries (mostly MNase and DNase I, plus CviJI, benzonase, dsDNA fragmentase, and quadRE) with different sequence cutting preferences. DNase-I only is the 65 of those 237 replicates ENCODE metadata confirms were digested with DNase I specifically, merged the same additive way.

Generated from motif_accessibility_profiles, nucleosome_detection, and their DNase-I-only regenerated counterparts.
