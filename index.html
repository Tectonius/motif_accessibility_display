GM12878 TF Accessibility Profiles

Aggregate accessibility signal around transcription factor binding sites in GM12878 — motif-centered analysis from a pre-annotated ChIP-seq peak source, plus an independent real MNase-seq replication check

About these figures

Aggregate profile (top)

Generated with deepTools computeMatrix reference-point + plotProfile. The x-axis is distance in bp from the reference point (0 = the reference point itself — see below for what that point is in each analysis). The y-axis is the mean signal value, averaged across every region in the set, at that position.

Heatmap (bottom)

Same underlying matrix as the profile plot, rendered with plotHeatmap instead of averaged down to one line. Same x-axis (distance from the reference point). Each row is one individual region, one ChIP-seq peak, and/or one motif instance. Color encodes that specific region's signal intensity at that bin according to the color bar on the right. Rows are sorted top-to-bottom by descending mean signal (deepTools' default --sortRegions descend --sortUsing mean), so the top rows are the individual sites with the strongest, most consistent signal, fading to the weakest/noisiest sites at the bottom.

Annotated motif analysis

Regions are motif instances for the selected TF from a pre-annotated ChIP-seq peak source (ENCODE_intact_Hi-C_shared_data/ChIP-Seq/GM12878) where each peak already carries whether it contains a high-confidence motif hit, that motif's coordinates, and its strand -- no separate JASPAR-genome-scan-plus-bedtools-intersect step needed, and substantially higher hit rates than a from-scratch scan-and-intersect pipeline would give (median ~40% of peaks with a hit). Centered on the motif's own predicted location, ±3000bp flank, 25bp bins. Of the 167 genes available in this source, 73 are histone marks, Pol II phospho-isoforms, or general chromatin cofactors (e.g. H3K27ac, EP300, RAD21) with no sequence-specific motif at all, so this analysis covers the 94 genes with at least one high-confidence hit.

Full, CTCF co-binding excluded

A motif instance is dropped if any CTCF ChIP-seq peak center (union of all 4 CTCF replicate peak files, reused from earlier CTCF-exclusion work) falls within its own +/-3000bp aggregation window -- not just literal overlap, so a CTCF site nearby but not directly overlapping the motif still counts as confounding co-binding. Guards against mistaking CTCF's own strong nucleosome- phasing signal (CTCF is the strongest known phaser of nucleosomes in the genome) for a TF's own effect, when the two happen to bind near each other. CTCF itself isn't part of this track. The fraction excluded varies a lot by TF -- e.g. ZNF143 loses 73% of its motif instances to CTCF proximity, YY1 more than half -- worth checking per-TF before reading too much into a small surviving n.

MNase-seq analysis

A peak-center-referenced aggregate (same method as Annotated motif analysis's peak-based tracks), on a fresh, larger peak set (155 non-CTCF, non-histone-mark targets from ENCODE_intact_Hi-C_shared_data/ChIP-Seq/GM12878) with every peak that has a CTCF peak center within its own ±2000bp aggregation window removed first (not just literal footprint overlap with CTCF -- any CTCF site close enough to potentially contribute to the flanking signal; CTCF is the strongest known positioner of phased nucleosomes in the human genome, so this guards against attributing its signal to a nearby TF instead). Aggregated against a genuinely independent signal source: ENCODE ENCSR000CXP, a real, standalone MNase-seq experiment on GM12878 (Snyder lab/Stanford, single-end SOLiD sequencing, released 2011), lifted from hg19 to hg38 to match every other track here -- this track shares no reads with any of the Hi-C-derived tracks, so it's a genuine orthogonal replication check, not just internal consistency. Caveat: this dataset is far lower-throughput and much older than the Hi-C-derived tracks, and the hg19->hg38 liftOver introduces some coordinate-precision loss.

Result: oscillation scores from this track correlate significantly with the same CTCF-excluded peak set's Hi-C-derived full-track oscillation scores, across all 155 TFs (Spearman ρ = 0.336, p = 2.0×10-5; Pearson r = 0.498, p = 4.2×10-11) -- moderate, not perfect, agreement. Most top-ranked TFs there (BATF, BCL11A, SPI1, REST, IRF4, RUNX3, JUNB) also score highly here, several even higher (e.g. SPI1 0.594 → 0.809, RUNX3 0.431 → 0.781) -- real evidence the phasing signal isn't Hi-C-specific. The clearest outlier is SMARCA5 (0.375 on the Hi-C-derived track vs. 0.091 here), worth treating with caution given the two disagree sharply on it. See the full comparison scatter plot for all 155 TFs.

Hi-C vs. MNase-seq comparison (CTCF-excluded)

Overlays the two CTCF-excluded aggregate profiles for the same TF on one chart, instead of comparing their oscillation scores as a single number: the Hi-C h.bw signal (blue) and the real MNase-seq signal (orange), both from the identical 155-TF, CTCF-proximal-peak-excluded region set and identical deepTools parameters (peak-center reference point, ±2000bp, 10bp bins), so the x-axis lines up exactly between the two. The two signals sit on very different scales (h.bw baseline ~40-60, MNase-seq baseline ~0.85-1.0), so each gets its own y-axis -- Hi-C on the left in blue, MNase-seq on the right in orange, matching their line colors -- rather than forcing both onto one shared scale where the smaller-magnitude signal would look flat. This is a single overlay chart, not a deepTools profile/heatmap pair -- there's no heatmap for it (the heatmap panel will say "not available" when this analysis is selected, same as the strand-comparison tracks above). No independent oscillation ranking either -- sort by TF name; MNase-seq analysis's own ctcf_excluded track carries its real MNase-seq oscillation score, and the Spearman/Pearson comparison numbers above (and top-TF score table) summarize how the two tracks' scores relate quantitatively.

Full vs. DNase-I only vs. MNase-only track

All three tracks are the same kind of measurement, a base-pair-resolution pileup of enzymatic cut/insertion events, additively pooled across GM12878 Hi-C replicates. Full is all 237 pooled replicates, which span at least six different fragmentation chemistries (mostly MNase and DNase I, plus CviJI, benzonase, dsDNA fragmentase, and quadRE) with different sequence cutting preferences. DNase-I only is the sum of 65 of those 237 replicates that ENCODE metadata confirms was digested with DNase I specifically. MNase only is the sum of 70 of those 237 replicates that ENCODE metadata confirms was digested with micrococcal nuclease instead.

Strand-split tracks (+ strand / − strand, full and DNase-I only)

Motif instances are split by the strand already recorded for each motif hit in the pre-annotated ChIP-seq peak source (not a ChIP-seq peak strand — narrowPeak entries don't carry one) before aggregating. deepTools orients minus-strand regions when building the matrix, so both tracks already plot in a consistent 5'→3' direction relative to the motif — a "+ strand" instance and a "− strand" instance of the same motif are shown the same way round, not mirror images of each other by construction. So a real difference between the two strand tracks reflects genuine strand-linked asymmetry (biological or technical) in the flanking accessibility signal, not a coordinate artifact. Not yet computed for the MNase-only track. A gene with too few motif instances on one strand may have no plot for that "− strand" (or, rarely, "+ strand") view.

Strand comparison (full and DNase-I only)
