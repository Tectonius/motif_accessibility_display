About these figures

Aggregate profile (top)

Generated with deepTools computeMatrix reference-point + plotProfile. The x-axis is distance in bp from the reference point (0 = the reference point itself — see below for what that point is in each analysis). The y-axis is the mean signal value, averaged across every region in the set, at that position.

Heatmap (bottom)

Same underlying matrix as the profile plot, rendered with plotHeatmap instead of averaged down to one line. Same x-axis (distance from the reference point). Each row is one individual region, one ChIP-seq peak, and/or one motif instance. Color encodes that specific region's signal intensity at that bin according to the color bar on the right. Rows are sorted top-to-bottom by descending mean signal (deepTools' default --sortRegions descend --sortUsing mean), so the top rows are the individual sites with the strongest, most consistent signal, fading to the weakest/noisiest sites at the bottom.

Motif accessibility vs. Nucleosome detection

Motif accessibility: regions are JASPAR motif instances for the selected TF, filtered to only the instances that overlap a real ChIP-seq peak for that TF ("motif-verified ChIP-seq peaks") centered on the motif's own predicted location, ±3000bp flank, 25bp bins. Nucleosome detection: regions are the TF's raw ChIP-seq peaks with no motif filtering, centered on the peak's own summit. Because the reference point, flank, and bin size all differ between the two, they aren't directly comparable position-for-position, and their oscillation scores are computed independently.

Annotated motif analysis / Annotated non-motif analysis

Both use a different ChIP-seq peak source (ENCODE_intact_Hi-C_shared_data/ChIP-Seq/GM12878) where each peak is already annotated with whether it contains a high-confidence motif hit, its coordinates, and its strand -- no separate JASPAR-genome-scan-plus-bedtools-intersect step needed, and substantially higher hit rates than the from-scratch pipeline above (median ~40% of peaks with a hit here, vs. single digits for most TFs in Motif accessibility). Of the 167 genes available in this source, 73 are histone marks, Pol II phospho-isoforms, or general chromatin cofactors (e.g. H3K27ac, EP300, RAD21) with no sequence-specific motif at all -- real ChIP-seq experiments with real peaks, just nothing to center a motif view on. Annotated motif analysis covers the 94 genes with at least one high-confidence hit, centered on the motif itself (same parameters as Motif accessibility). Annotated non-motif analysis covers all 167 genes, centered on the raw peak (same parameters as Nucleosome detection) -- deliberately motif-agnostic, so it's the only place here where the histone marks and other non-motif targets show up. Full track (h.bw) only so far.

Full, CTCF co-binding excluded (Annotated motif analysis only)

A motif instance is dropped if any CTCF ChIP-seq peak center (union of all 4 CTCF replicate peak files, reused from the CTCF-excluded Nucleosome detection work) falls within its own +/-3000bp aggregation window -- not just literal overlap, so a CTCF site nearby but not directly overlapping the motif still counts as confounding co-binding. Guards against mistaking CTCF's own strong nucleosome- phasing signal (CTCF is the strongest known phaser of nucleosomes in the genome) for a TF's own effect, when the two happen to bind near each other. CTCF itself isn't part of this track. The fraction excluded varies a lot by TF -- e.g. ZNF143 loses 73% of its motif instances to CTCF proximity, YY1 more than half -- worth checking per-TF before reading too much into a small surviving n.

Nucleosome detection, CTCF-excluded

CTCF is the strongest known positioner of phased nucleosomes in the human genome, so any nucleosome-phasing ripple seen at a TF's ChIP-seq peaks could really be coming from a nearby CTCF site rather than the TF itself. This analysis re-runs the same peak-center-referenced method as Nucleosome detection, but on a fresh, larger peak set (155 non-CTCF, non-histone-mark targets from ENCODE_intact_Hi-C_shared_data/ChIP-Seq/GM12878) with every peak that has a CTCF peak center within its own +/-2000bp aggregation window removed first (not just literal footprint overlap with CTCF -- any CTCF site close enough to potentially contribute to the flanking signal). Two tracks: Full (the same h.bw used elsewhere) and MNase only (the Hi-C MNase-cutter subset) -- both still Hi-C-derived, so close agreement between them mainly reflects that they share most of the same underlying sequencing reads, not independent confirmation.

Nucleosome detection, CTCF-excluded (real MNase-seq)

The same CTCF-excluded 155-TF peak set as above, but aggregated against a genuinely independent signal source: ENCODE ENCSR000CXP, a real, standalone MNase-seq experiment on GM12878 (Snyder lab/Stanford, single-end SOLiD sequencing, released 2011), lifted from hg19 to hg38 to match every other track here. This is the orthogonal replication check -- unlike the Full/MNase-only tracks above, this track shares no reads with the Hi-C data, so agreement between this and the CTCF-excluded Full track is real evidence that non-CTCF nucleosome phasing isn't a Hi-C- or CTCF-specific artifact. Caveat: this dataset is far lower-throughput and much older than the Hi-C-derived tracks, and the hg19->hg38 liftOver introduces some coordinate-precision loss -- both worth weighing when interpreting any disagreement.

Result: oscillation scores from this track and the CTCF-excluded Full track correlate significantly across all 155 TFs (Spearman ρ = 0.336, p = 2.0×10-5; Pearson r = 0.498, p = 4.2×10-11) -- moderate, not perfect, agreement. Most top-ranked TFs on the Full track (BATF, BCL11A, SPI1, REST, IRF4, RUNX3, JUNB) also score highly here, several even higher (e.g. SPI1 0.594 → 0.809, RUNX3 0.431 → 0.781) -- real evidence the phasing signal isn't Hi-C-specific. The clearest outlier is SMARCA5 (0.375 on Full vs. 0.091 here), worth treating with caution given the two tracks disagree sharply on it. See the full comparison scatter plot for all 155 TFs.

Full vs. DNase-I only vs. MNase-only track

All three tracks are the same kind of measurement, a base-pair-resolution pileup of enzymatic cut/insertion events, additively pooled across GM12878 Hi-C replicates. Full is all 237 pooled replicates, which span at least six different fragmentation chemistries (mostly MNase and DNase I, plus CviJI, benzonase, dsDNA fragmentase, and quadRE) with different sequence cutting preferences. DNase-I only is the sum of 65 of those 237 replicates that ENCODE metadata confirms was digested with DNase I specifically. MNase only is the sum of 70 of those 237 replicates that ENCODE metadata confirms was digested with micrococcal nuclease instead.

Strand-split tracks (+ strand / − strand, full and DNase-I only)

The motif instances are split by the strand of the JASPAR motif-scan hit itself (not a ChIP-seq peak strand — narrowPeak entries here don't carry one) before aggregating. deepTools orients minus-strand regions when building the matrix, so both tracks already plot in a consistent 5'→3' direction relative to the motif — a "+ strand" instance and a "− strand" instance of the same motif are shown the same way round, not mirror images of each other by construction. So a real difference between the two strand tracks reflects genuine strand-linked asymmetry (biological or technical) in the flanking accessibility signal, not a coordinate artifact. For Motif accessibility this is the motif-centered pipeline as usual; for Nucleosome detection (which has no strand of its own — raw ChIP-seq peaks aren't stranded) this instead reuses the same ChIP-confirmed motif instances, split by strand, but aggregated with Nucleosome detection's own window/bin parameters, so its region set (and N) is narrower than the base "chip peaks only" track. Not yet computed for the MNase-only track. Two TFs (FOS, RXRA) have too few motif-verified peaks to produce a minus-strand instance at all, so their "− strand" view has no plot, on either analysis or track.

Strand comparison (full and DNase-I only)

Overlays the + strand and − strand aggregate profiles for the same TF on one chart, instead of viewing each separately — the fastest way to see whether the two strand-split tracks above actually differ from each other. Available for both Motif accessibility and Nucleosome detection.
